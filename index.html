Asset Inference Algorithm Atlas

Algorithms for inferring what an industrial asset is from its sensor tags, and for optimising and controlling it once known. Built around a thesis that classifies signals and infers system type from input-output behaviour, with no declared asset metadata. Model-free methods are marked.

179 algorithms13 families11 asset types mapped

Suggested order of attack

  1. Prepare. Align and clean tags, detect signal type, and cut data into steady-state, step and mode segments (section 1).
  2. Find inputs. Rank candidate input tags per output with lagged cross-correlation, transfer entropy and PCMCI, and read writable and unit metadata from OPC UA (section 3).
  3. Fingerprint. Fit a bank of candidate models (lag, integrator, delay, double integrator, nonlinear) in closed-loop-safe form, record the winning structure and its parameters (section 4).
  4. Classify. Combine fingerprint, spectral and process-response features with MiniRocket or catch22, then train a gradient-boosted or random-forest model (sections 2 and 5).
  5. Place in the taxonomy. Use hierarchical classification with probability propagation, conformal sets and out-of-distribution detection so uncertain assets stop at a parent class (sections 5 and 6).
  6. Act. Map the type to a tuning rule or model-free optimiser, wrapped in a safety layer (sections 10 to 13 and the mapping table).
PhD fit is my judgement for a thesis on asset-type inference. Core: likely part of the main pipeline. Useful: worth benchmarking. Adjacent: background or later work. Kind says whether a method needs a process model.

1. Signal preparation and segmentation 10

Raw OPC UA and SCADA tags are irregular, noisy and mixed in type. These steps decide whether any later inference is trustworthy.

PhD fit
Time alignment and resampling
Tags arrive at irregular times, often by exception. Resample to a grid with zero-order hold, x(t) = x(tₖ) for tₖ ≤ t < tₖ₊₁, or linear interpolation. Report-by-exception data needs hold, not smoothing.

Steady-state detection
Cao-Rhinehart R-statistic compares a variance estimate from filtered deviations with one from successive differences; R ≈ 1 at steady state, larger in transients. Alternatives: moving-window slope t-test, wavelet detectors.

CUSUM, Sₜ = max(0, Sₜ₋₁ + xₜ − μ₀ − k). PELT minimises Σ cost + β·(#changes) with pruned exact search. Bayesian online change-point detection. Binary segmentation. Setpoint steps and mode switches are the most informative moments for input-output inference.

3. Discovering input-output relationships 16

Which tag drives which, with what delay and strength. This is the step that exposes a system's structure without declared metadata.

Algorithm
Idea and maths
Kind
PhD fit
Pearson and Spearman correlation
r = cov(X,Y)/(σₓσᵧ). Spearman uses ranks and catches monotone nonlinear links. Cheap first screen, blind to delay and direction.
Statistical
Useful
Lagged cross-correlation
R_xy(τ) = E[x(t) y(t+τ)]. The lag at the peak estimates transport delay, and the sign of the best lag hints at which tag leads.
Statistical
Core
Pre-whitened cross-correlation (Box-Jenkins)
Fit an AR model to the input, filter both signals with it, then cross-correlate. The result is proportional to the impulse response even when the input is coloured.
Statistical
Core
Mutual information
I(X;Y) = ΣΣ p(x,y) log[p(x,y)/(p(x)p(y))]. Captures nonlinear dependence. Compute at several lags. Estimators: histogram, kNN (Kraskov), MINE.
Statistical
Core
Distance correlation and HSIC
Kernel and distance based dependence measures that are zero only under independence. Robust to nonlinearity and dimension.
Statistical
Useful
Magnitude-squared coherence
C_xy(f) = |S_xy(f)|² / (S_xx(f) S_yy(f)). Shows in which frequency band two tags are linearly related.
Statistical
Useful
Granger causality
y is Granger-caused by x if past x reduces the prediction error of y beyond past y. F-test between restricted and full VAR models. Neural and nonlinear variants exist.
Statistical
Core
Transfer entropy
T_{X→Y} = Σ p(yₜ₊₁, yₜ, xₜ) log[p(yₜ₊₁|yₜ,xₜ)/p(yₜ₊₁|yₜ)]. The information-theoretic, nonlinear generalisation of Granger causality.
Statistical
Core
PCMCI / PCMCI+
Causal discovery for time series: PC-style condition selection followed by momentary conditional independence tests. Handles many tags and autocorrelation. Implemented in Tigramite.
Statistical
Core
Convergent cross mapping
Tests causality in coupled nonlinear dynamical systems by checking whether the shadow manifold of Y predicts X.
Statistical
Adjacent
Partial correlation and graphical lasso
Estimate a sparse precision matrix Θ = Σ⁻¹ by minimising −log det Θ + tr(SΘ) + λ‖Θ‖₁. Non-zero entries are direct links with confounders removed.
Statistical
Useful
Dynamic time warping
DTW(x,y) = min over warping paths of Σ d(xᵢ, yⱼ). Compares signals with varying delay or speed. Used both for similarity and as a kNN distance.
Statistical
Useful
Cointegration (Engle-Granger, Johansen)
Detects long-run linear relations between non-stationary tags, for example a level and an integrated flow.
Statistical
Adjacent
Event-triggered cross-correlation
Detect change events in each tag, then correlate event times. Works for on/off and stepped actuators where continuous correlation is weak.
Data-driven
Core
Sparse regression of dependencies
Lasso or elastic net regressing each tag on lagged others, min ‖y − Xβ‖² + λ‖β‖₁. The non-zero coefficients give a candidate input set.

4. System identification as a fingerprint 21

Fit candidate dynamic models and use the structure and parameters as the asset's signature. SCADA data is usually closed-loop, so check each method's closed-loop validity.

Algorithm
Idea and maths
Kind
PhD fit
FOPDT / SOPDT step-response fitting
G(s) = K e^{−θs}/(τs+1). Fit gain K, time constant τ and delay θ from a setpoint or step response. Second-order plus delay adds damping ratio. The tuple (K, τ, θ) is a compact fingerprint.
Model-based
Core
Integrating and unstable process fits
G(s) = K e^{−θs}/s, typical of tanks and levels. Distinguish self-regulating from integrating by whether y settles after a constant input.
Model-based
Core
Reaction-curve and area methods
Ziegler-Nichols reaction curve, Smith, Sundaresan, area and moment methods for estimating FOPDT parameters from a single step.
Model-based
Useful
Correlation analysis (Wiener-Hopf)
ĝ = R_uu⁻¹ R_uy, impulse response from input autocovariance and cross-covariance. Needs input persistent excitation.
Data-driven
Useful
FIR and impulse-response models
y(t) = Σ gₖ u(t−k), fitted by least squares with regularisation (ridge, kernel / stable-spline methods).
Data-driven
Useful
ARX
A(q)y(t) = B(q)u(t−nₖ) + e(t). Linear in parameters: θ = (ΦᵀΦ)⁻¹Φᵀy. Fast to fit a bank of orders and delays. Biased in closed loop if noise is coloured.
Data-driven
Core
ARMAX, Output-Error, Box-Jenkins, PEM
Prediction-error minimisation θ̂ = argmin Σ ε(t,θ)² with richer noise models. OE gives unbiased plant estimates in open loop. PEM is the general framework.

Core
Recursive least squares
θₖ = θₖ₋₁ + Kₖ(yₖ − φₖᵀθₖ₋₁), Kₖ = Pₖ₋₁φₖ/(λ + φₖᵀPₖ₋₁φₖ). Online identification with forgetting factor λ.
Data-driven

Subspace methods (N4SID, MOESP, CVA)
Estimate state-space (A,B,C,D) from Hankel matrices of I/O data using SVD. No iterative search, handles MIMO.
Data-driven
Core
Frequency-domain estimation
Empirical transfer function estimate Ĝ(jω) = Φ_yu(ω)/Φ_uu(ω), then rational fitting (vector fitting, Loewner).
Data-driven
Useful
Closed-loop identification schemes
Direct, indirect, joint input-output and two-stage methods, and instrumental variables. Required because most plant data is collected under PID or PLC control.
Data-driven
Core
Hammerstein, Wiener, Hammerstein-Wiener
Static nonlinearity before and/or after linear dynamics. Fits valves, saturating actuators and sensors.
Data-driven
Useful
NARX / NARMAX

Data-driven
Useful
SINDy
Sparse identification of nonlinear dynamics: ẋ = Θ(x)ξ, solve sparse ξ by sequentially thresholded least squares. Returns interpretable equations.
Data-driven
Useful
DMD, EDMD and Koopman methods
DMD fits xₖ₊₁ ≈ Axₖ from snapshots. EDMD lifts states with a dictionary so nonlinear dynamics become linear in the lifted space.
Data-driven
Useful
Neural ODE and recurrent identification
dx/dt = f_θ(x,u) integrated by an ODE solver, or LSTM/GRU/TCN sequence models trained on I/O data.
Data-driven
Useful

Nonparametric dynamics with calibrated uncertainty. Useful when data is scarce.
Data-driven
Adjacent
Order and structure selection
AIC = 2k − 2 ln L̂, BIC = k ln n − 2 ln L̂, MDL, cross-validation. The best-scoring model class is itself a taxonomy feature.
Statistical

Fit a bank of candidate structures (first-order lag, integrator, delay-dominant, double integrator, nonlinear) and choose by information criterion or posterior probability. Bayesian form: P(Mᵢ|D) ∝ P(D|Mᵢ)P(Mᵢ). Multiple-model adaptive estimation runs one Kalman filter per model.

6. Taxonomy, ontology and semantic methods 11

Place the inferred type in a hierarchy and reconcile it with industrial vocabularies.

Algorithm
Idea and maths
Kind
PhD fit
Flat vs hierarchical classification
Flat ignores structure. Local classifier per node (LCN), per parent node (LCPN) or per level, and global single-model approaches. Top-down inference can compound errors, so combine with probability propagation P(child) = P(child|parent)P(parent).
Supervised
Core
Hierarchy-aware losses and metrics
Hierarchical softmax, tree-distance-weighted loss, hierarchical precision/recall/F-measure, lowest-common-ancestor error. A misclassification between sibling types should cost less than one across branches.
Supervised
Core
Hierarchical multi-label classification
An asset can belong to several ancestors at once (rotating machine, actuator, electric drive). Enforce the hierarchy constraint so predicting a child implies its parents.
Supervised
Core
Formal concept analysis
Derive a concept lattice from an object-attribute table. Builds a taxonomy bottom-up from measured attributes such as has delay, is integrating, is bounded.
Semantic

10. Model-free and black-box self-optimisation 22

Optimise using only measured cost J(θ). These are the main candidates for the self-optimisation part of your work.

Algorithm
Idea and maths
Kind
PhD fit
Finite-difference / Kiefer-Wolfowitz
ĝᵢ = [J(θ + cₖeᵢ) − J(θ − cₖeᵢ)]/(2cₖ), then θ ← θ − aₖĝ. Needs 2n evaluations per step.
Model-free
Core
SPSA
Perturb all parameters together with random ±1 vector Δ: ĝₖ = [J(θ+cΔ) − J(θ−cΔ)]/(2c) · Δ⁻¹. Two evaluations per step regardless of dimension. Gains aₖ = a/(k+A)^α, cₖ = c/k^γ.
Model-free
Core
Extremum-seeking control
Dither θ = θ̂ + a sin ωt, measure y = J(θ), demodulate and integrate: dθ̂/dt = −k y sin ωt. On average dθ̂/dt ≈ −(ka/2)∇J.
Model-free
Core
Perturb and observe / hill climbing
Step θ → θ + δ. Keep direction if J improves, reverse otherwise. Used for MPPT.
Model-free
Useful
Nelder-Mead simplex
Keep n+1 points. Reflect worst through centroid: θᵣ = c̄ + α(c̄ − θ_worst), then expand, contract or shrink.
Model-free
Useful
Pattern search, Powell, Rosenbrock, DIRECT, MADS
Derivative-free direct-search families with convergence theory for mesh-based variants.
Model-free
Adjacent
Simulated annealing
Accept θ' with probability min(1, exp(−ΔJ/T)), with T cooled slowly.

vᵢ ← wvᵢ + c₁r₁(pᵢ − θᵢ) + c₂r₂(g − θᵢ), θᵢ ← θᵢ + vᵢ. Constriction χ = 2/|2−φ−√(φ²−4φ)|. Variants: inertia decay, MOPSO. Typical use: offline PID or MPC weight tuning on simulation or twin.
Model-free
Core

GA: selection, crossover, mutation. DE: v = xᵣ₁ + F(xᵣ₂ − xᵣ₃), binomial crossover, greedy selection.
Model-free
Useful

Sample θ ~ N(m, σ²C), rank by J, update m and covariance C. Natural-gradient estimate ∇J ≈ (1/Nσ)Σ J(θ+σεᵢ)εᵢ.

Gaussian-process surrogate, next point argmax EI(x) with EI = E[max(0, f* − f(x))]. Sample-efficient for expensive experiments. Black-box rather than strictly model-free.

14. Asset type to method map

The dynamic fingerprint to expect for common asset types, and where it points in the algorithm list. Use as hypotheses to test, not facts.

Electric motor / drive (voltage or command in, speed out)
Expect a first- or second-order lag with high bandwidth, near-linear gain, saturation at limits, rotating spectral content. Fit ARX/OE or FOPDT, check spectral peaks. Controllers: PI, FOC cascade; tune with VRFT or SIMC; ESC for efficiency.
Tank or vessel level (inflow or valve in, level out)
Integrating process: output ramps under constant input difference, no steady state. Test with integrator-plus-delay fit and the model-bank criterion. Controllers: P or PI with anti-windup, level-averaging control.
Heater, furnace, thermal loop
Large time constant and dead time, small gain per unit input, one-sided actuation. FOPDT with θ/τ large, strong low-frequency spectrum. Controllers: PID with lambda tuning, Smith predictor, MPC.
Flow loop
Fast first-order lag, noisy measurement, valve nonlinearity. Look for stiction and deadband signatures. Controllers: PI, with stiction compensation or valve positioner diagnostics.
Pressure loop
Self-regulating or integrating depending on volume and leakage. Short time constants for gases. Test both structures and select by information criterion.
Control valve / actuator
Static nonlinearity (installed characteristic), hysteresis, stiction, rate limits. Hammerstein model, u-y hysteresis loop features, stiction detectors.
Servo axis (Beckhoff / TwinCAT motion)
Double-integrator-like position response, velocity and position tags related by integration (x = ∫v). Cross-tag integration check is a strong type cue. Controllers: cascade P-PI with feedforward, ADRC, ILC for repetitive motions.
Conveyor or transport line
Pure transport delay dominates, y(t) = K u(t − θ). Cross-correlation peak at a clear lag with little dynamics after it.
Pump or fan
Nonlinear gain following affinity laws (flow ∝ speed, head ∝ speed²), first-order speed lag. Check power-law relation between tags.
Discrete devices (cylinders, valves on/off, counters)
Boolean or enum tags. Use automata learning, event-triggered correlation, duty-cycle and dwell-time features rather than continuous system identification.
Batch or sequenced unit
Repeating phases with recipe-driven setpoints. Segment into phases, use HMM or automata learning, ILC or run-to-run control between batches.
